About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Physics at Boston College and a graduate researcher in the Schiller Institute for Integrated Science and Society, where my advisor is Dr. Yi Ming. My research focuses on atmospheric dynamics and idealized climate modeling, with an emphasis on how tropical divergent circulations—such as the Hadley and Walker cells—respond to zonally asymmetric thermal forcings and elevated CO2 concentrations. To isolate the fundamental physical mechanisms governing these atmospheric responses, I utilize a combination of idealized moist general circulation models (GCMs), diagnostic physical frameworks, CMIP6 model ensembles, and reanalysis data (ERA5) on high-performance computing clusters.

I earned my B.S. in Physics with a minor in Mathematics in 2020 from The Ohio State University (Magna Cum Laude, with Research Distinction in Physics). My broader background spans several areas of theoretical and computational physics: at OSU, I conducted research on non-linear dynamical systems and stochastic PDEs with Dr. Ciriyam Jayaprakash, physics education research with Dr. Andrew Heckler and Dr. Gregory Kilcup, and experimental 2D materials with Dr. Jay Gupta. Before joining Dr. Ming’s group, I also worked on non-Hermitian second-quantized systems in theoretical condensed matter physics with Dr. Benedetta Flebus at Boston College.
